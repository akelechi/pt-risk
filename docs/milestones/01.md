## What I expected, what happened, what I learned

**Expected (first cut, Henry Hub flat):** with Henry Hub near $4 and January LBMPs above
$100, the unit would run most hours and the margin would be very large.

**Happened (first cut):** it ran 92% of hours at essentially full output and stopped for
price only on 17-18 January, when Henry Hub printed $9.86.

**Expected (with the assumed $3 basis):** fewer hours and a lower margin, with the unit
out of the money in the mild stretches of the month.

**Happened:** the margin fell by $10.9M (37%). The unit ran 551 hours, not 686, and sat
out three stretches (1-4, 11-14 and 17-19 January). Starts doubled from 7 to 14, so the
milestone 2 start-cost work now has more to bite on.

**Learned:**

1. **The fuel price is the largest lever in the model.** A flat $3/MMBtu adder moved the
   month by $10.9M; the market's implied heat rate fell from 28.8 to 16.5 MMBtu/MWh
   against a unit heat rate near 10.5. That is still a wide spread, and the output is
   still out of scale with history: EIA-923 reports 236,658 MWh for both units over all
   of 2024, and this run has one unit producing 303,846 MWh in one month. A flat adder
   cannot follow the days when delivered gas spikes with the power price; milestone 5's
   published index is what closes the rest of the gap.
2. **Availability is the largest lever among the plant parameters** (+$3.2M with no
   derate, measured at Henry Hub flat), because the unit is at its cap almost every hour. Heat rate (-$1.8M at the
   EIA level) and capacity (-$0.7M at the brief's 600 MW) matter less here, for the same
   reason: at these spreads nearly every MWh is deep in the money.
3. **The convexified curve has only two steps.** Pooling 11.4 / 10.1 / 10.0 into one step
   means the unit goes from 0 to 450 MW in one move; it produced at 450 MW in just 3 hours.
4. **Fourteen starts were free.** Start cost, no-load and minimum run times arrive in
   milestone 2; the 10 hours at 450 MW and the short stops are where the DP solver should
   differ from this baseline.
5. **Offer prices need a tick.** Pooled heat rates are not exact in floating point, so a
   price that ties the offer could clear or not by rounding noise. Offers are now rounded
   to the cent (market config), which makes "strictly above" well defined.
