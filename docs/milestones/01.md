## What I expected, what happened, what I learned

**Expected:** with Henry Hub near $4 and January LBMPs above $100, the unit would run most
hours and the margin would be very large; the question was how large and when it would
stop.

**Happened:** it ran 92% of hours at essentially full output. The only time it stopped
for price was 17-18 January, when Henry Hub printed $9.86 and the first offer step rose to
$106.25. That is the one moment in the month where the fuel price mattered at all.

**Learned:**

1. **Henry Hub flat is the wrong fuel price for this plant, and the error is the whole
   answer.** The market's implied heat rate (LBMP / Henry Hub) averaged 28.8 MMBtu/MWh in
   January against a unit heat rate near 10.5. Nothing clears at that spread in a
   competitive market: the gap is delivered gas basis into the Hudson Valley in winter.
   For scale, EIA-923 reports 236,658 MWh for both units over all of 2024; this run has
   one unit producing 379,268 MWh in one month. Milestone 5 (delivered basis) will move
   this number more than any other milestone. Until then every margin from this engine
   should be read as "against Henry Hub".
2. **Availability is the largest lever among the plant parameters** (+$3.2M with no
   derate), because the unit is at its cap almost every hour. Heat rate (-$1.8M at the
   EIA level) and capacity (-$0.7M at the brief's 600 MW) matter less here, for the same
   reason: at these spreads nearly every MWh is deep in the money.
3. **The convexified curve has only two steps.** Pooling 11.4 / 10.1 / 10.0 into one step
   means the unit goes from 0 to 450 MW in one move; it produced at 450 MW in just 3 hours.
4. **Seven starts were free.** Start cost, no-load and minimum run times arrive in
   milestone 2; the 3 hours at 450 MW and the short stops on 1-2 January are where the DP
   solver should differ from this baseline.
5. **Offer prices need a tick.** Pooled heat rates are not exact in floating point, so a
   price that ties the offer could clear or not by rounding noise. Offers are now rounded
   to the cent (market config), which makes "strictly above" well defined.
