## What I expected, what happened, what I learned

**Expected:** about three outage days in the month, each costing roughly a day's margin
(about $0.6M), and a total near the 10% derate.

**Happened:** three days were drawn but two fell on days the unit was off, so one day
carried the whole cost, and that one day cost $1.41M: more than twice a typical day's
margin.

**Learned:**

1. **An outage costs more than the margin it removes.** On 24 January the lost margin
   was $0.97M; buying the schedule back at RT prices above DA added $0.41M, and the
   restart $36k. A derate cannot show the last two.
2. **The derate overstated the cost in this month, and by a lot.** $1.97M deterministic
   against a mean of $1.24M over 50 draws. The derate takes 10% off every hour including
   the best ones; a drawn outage often lands on a day the unit would not have run.
   Fifty seeds is a small sample, and the mean number of outage days drawn (2.62) is
   below the expected 3.1, so the gap is indicative.
3. **An outage can be a gain.** One seed came out $100k better with its outage than
   without: the schedule was bought back at an RT price below the unit's cost.
4. **The spread is the point.** From a $100k gain to a $3.65M loss in one month, from
   the same probability. Milestone 8 is where this becomes a distribution.
5. **Uplift needed a rule I had not written.** A block cut short by an outage would
   otherwise have been made whole for the unit's own failure (Q-037).

## Changed from milestone 2

* `transition` separates the day-ahead schedule from actual output; state follows what
  physically happened. `HourInputs` carries the RT LBMP and the outage flag.
* `Scenario.availability` (`derated` or `full`) for runs without outage draws.
* Invariant 4 compares the sum of each revenue term rounded to the cent, exactly.
* New rules: `docs/methodology.md` §14. New questions: Q-035 to Q-038.
* The milestone 1 and 2 numbers are unchanged; tests pin both.

## UI view touched

Flow screen: the RT market node shows the RT LBMP; the deviation arrow shows the MW
shortfall and the RT balancing arrow and ledger line show the buy-back in dollars; on an
outage hour the plant node carries an OUTAGE marker (dashed frame, crossed box and the
word), the decision line shows the day-ahead plan and what happened in real time, and
the commitment timeline marks outage days above the run's own track. Screenshots:
