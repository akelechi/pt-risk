## What I expected, what happened, what I learned

**Expected:** with oil near $12 and gas near $7.60 on average, oil would matter only on
the Henry Hub spike around 17-21 January, perhaps four or five days, and would let the
unit run through days the gas-only DP sat out.

**Happened:** oil was cheaper on exactly four days, but it was burned on two. On the 17th
the unit was off on either fuel (oil at $12.28 was only 4.5% cheaper than gas, not enough
to make the day pay), and the 18th was a drawn outage. The captured margin, $158k, is
0.9% of the month.

**Learned:**

1. **The value of oil here is small because the gas price is too kind.** With a flat $3
   basis, gas only exceeds oil when Henry Hub itself spikes. Real winter basis into the
   Hudson Valley spikes on the cold days when Henry Hub does not, which is exactly when
   an oil tank earns its keep. Milestone 5 should raise this number a lot; until then
   "days on oil" is close to a lower bound.
2. **The assumed 0.70 ratio decides everything.** At 0.74 oil would not have been cheaper
   on any day of this month; at 0.60 it would be far more attractive. There is no public
   daily No. 6 price to pin it (Q-039).
3. **Being cheaper is not the same as running.** Two of the four oil days produced
   nothing, for reasons unrelated to fuel. A fuel-choice count overstates use.
4. **Inventory and permit never bound.** 39 of 720 permit hours and 18% of the tank's
   target level were used. The constraints are tested on hand cases, not by this month.
5. **All eight invariants are now live.** Invariant 2 needed the state to record each
   hour's delivery, otherwise the tank balance could not be recomputed independently.

  starting tank level.
